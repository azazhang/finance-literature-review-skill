# Output Modes for Finance Literature Review

Select the output mode best matched to the research objective:

---

## 1. Prioritized Reading List Mode
* **Best For**: Fast topic orientation, syllabus preparation, PhD comprehensive exam prep, seminar reading lists.
* **Core Structure**:
  * **Bedrock Published Papers**: Top 3 Finance and Top 5 Econ foundational articles establishing core theory and baseline empirical facts.
  * **Subfield Leaders**: High-impact field journal articles (*JFQA*, *RCFS*, *RAPS*, *JMCB*, *TAR*).
  * **Frontier Working Papers**: Tier 1 Working Papers (NBER, CEPR, Central Banks, R&Rs at Top 3/Top 5, AFA/WFA/NBER SI presentations) defining the modern frontier.
  * **Verification Badges**: Explicitly notes `[Full-Text Verified]`, `[Intro / Table Verified]`, or `[Abstract-Only Provisional]`.
  * **Reading Annotations**: 2–3 sentences per paper detailing the intellectual lineage role, core mechanism, empirical setting, and boundary condition.

---

## 2. Literature Matrix Mode
* **Best For**: Systematic comparison of empirical strategies, meta-evaluations, survey appendices, referee responses.
* **Core Structure**:
  * Populates the 16-column schema from [templates/literature-matrix.md](../templates/literature-matrix.md).
  * Explicitly extracts: Theme, Citation, Venue & Status, Venue Tier, Verification Level, Lineage Role, Research Question, Setting & Primary Data Infrastructure, Sample Period & Exclusions, Unit of Observation, Identification Strategy & Specific Shock, Econometric Nuance & Fixed Effects, Verified Finding & Magnitude, Mechanism, Boundary Condition (What It Leaves Open), and Positioning Value.
  * Enforces abstract quarantine: abstract-only rows must label empirical details as unverified.

---

## 3. Related Literature Section Mode
* **Best For**: Drafting the literature review of a working paper, journal submission, or dissertation chapter.
* **Core Structure**:
  * **Thematic / Mechanism-Based Subsections**: Drafts continuous, publication-ready academic prose grouped by economic transmission channel (never a paper-by-paper list).
  * **Intellectual Lineage Mapping**: Traces from theoretical originators to early empirical correlations to modern quasi-experimental benchmarks.
  * **Causal Identification Contrasts**: Explicitly contrasts methodologies using the 3-anchor introduction extractions (e.g., "While early studies rely on OLS cross-sectional regressions, recent work exploits exogenous shocks using staggered difference-in-differences...").
  * **Frontier Integration**: Seamlessly cites active Tier 1 working papers to position the paper within ongoing debates.
  * **Direct Differentiation Based on Boundary Conditions**: Explicitly articulates how the researcher's paper departs from prior work by exploiting what earlier papers left open (e.g., new setting, post-crisis regulatory window, higher-frequency data, micro-level contractual terms).

---

## 4. Gap Map & Dissertation Positioning Mode
* **Best For**: Finding viable paper topics, second-year PhD papers, dissertation chapter proposals, faculty grant proposals.
* **Core Structure**:
  * **What Is Settled vs. What Is Contested**: High-level map separating consensus empirical facts from conflicting evidence.
  * **Identification Gaps**: Unresolved endogeneity hurdles, flawed exclusion restrictions, or pre-modern DiD designs needing re-examination.
  * **Data & Institutional Gaps**: Novel datasets (e.g., machine learning text extraction of SEC filings, granular consumer credit data, loan-level registry data) or recent regulatory shocks (e.g., Basel III endgame, regional bank resolution rules, IRA green subsidies) that have not yet been evaluated.
  * **Systematic Boundary Aggregation**: Gathers the "What It Leaves Open" entries from the matrix to identify clear empirical frontiers.
  * **Specific Research Proposals**: 3–5 concrete research questions with proposed empirical settings, identification strategies, and target journal positioning.
