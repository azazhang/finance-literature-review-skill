# Finance Literature Review

**Answer the user's research question from primary evidence.** Discover the economically closest important work, actually read the evidence needed for each material claim, evaluate its inferential credibility, and synthesize what the literature teaches financial economics. Produce the requested analysis or manuscript prose; a search plan, abstract collection, or large matrix alone is not the deliverable.

## Source access precedes substantive claims

**Every consequential claim about a study must be traceable to an identifiable source passage, exhibit, or theoretical result the agent actually examined.** A paper's title, abstract, citation record, or retrieved-PDF filename does not establish its design, evidence, mechanism, or contribution. Do not call a paper “read,” “verified,” or “assessed” unless the relevant source content was opened and inspected.

For the closest and decision-relevant papers, obtain the relevant full text (published or explicitly identified working-paper version) and examine the actual research question, design or theory, main results and notes, and claimed contribution wherever they occur. **A decisive design, estimate, or novelty assertion requires an actual inspected locator for its supporting source**, not a guessed page/table label. Assess limitations and competing explanations using methods, additional results, or appendix as needed. **There are no universal introduction paragraph numbers or universal tables.** Three substantive anchors matter: **how the paper makes inference; what its evidence shows; what economic insight it adds.**

If a paper is inaccessible, **try legitimate full-text recovery**: publisher/accepted manuscript; author and institutional repositories; SSRN, NBER, CEPR, central-bank versions; open-access indexes and citation links; and any authorized library or user-connected documents. Check that the recovered version matches the cited work and whether findings changed. If a pivotal paper remains inaccessible, request its PDF from the researcher while continuing accessible work. Do not invent a missing design or treat the abstract as a substitute. See [source-verification.md](references/source-verification.md).

An abstract can support *“the authors report X in their abstract”* if X is stated there. It does not permit independent claims that X is causally established, that a mechanism is identified, or that the contribution is novel. Keep such papers provisional in reading lists; **do not use them as decisive evidence for novelty, identifying comparisons, or settled research gaps.**

## Discover the closest relevant scholarship

Start from the economic question, mechanism, and nearest known papers. Search recent and foundational work in core finance, relevant field journals, economics/accounting, and active working-paper channels. Use backward/forward citation links and recent versions to detect genuine nearest neighbors and competing findings. **Working papers are essential on frontier topics**, but NBER/CEPR series, conference appearances, R&R claims, institutional affiliations, and downloads are discovery signals, not evidence that a design is credible. Confirm publication, version, and status rather than repeating metadata from search snippets. Use [discovery.md](references/discovery.md) when broad coverage or working-paper retrieval is part of the task.

## Analyze four substantive questions

1. **What do the closest studies actually establish, and how credible is their evidence?** Reconstruct each paper's economic object, theoretical mechanism or empirical variation, sample/units, primary findings and magnitudes, and inferential limitations from the actual source.
2. **Where do their findings and mechanisms agree or conflict?** Compare economically comparable estimands and settings, and assess identification, measurement, sample selection, period, uncertainty, and alternative channels.
3. **What remains economically important and unresolved?** Consider contested predictions, unpersuasive identification, conflicting evidence, missing mechanisms, and consequential changes in institutions or data. A gap need not be something earlier authors explicitly list as a limitation.
4. **What does the user's research contribute relative to those findings?** Compare the closest papers directly and fairly; assess new inference, mechanisms, theory, or evidence. A new dataset, method, or sample window earns a contribution through what it changes economically. Avoid unsupported “first” claims.

Treat a paper's own abstract, contributions, and limitations as its authors' assertions requiring evaluation. Intellectual-lineage labels can help but need not be mutually exclusive. See [critical-synthesis.md](references/critical-synthesis.md).

## Source-grounded delivery

For each pivotal paper and material comparison, retain a **source-to-claim trail** (stable paper identity/version and the section, page, table, figure, equation, or equivalent locator supporting the point). **In substantive deep reviews, show those locators for pivotal design, finding, and contribution claims** in the analysis or concise source notes; never invent them. Check the completed synthesis against the sources again: no causal upgrading, invented estimates, unsupported mechanisms, misattributed priority, or unverified publication status. A locator alone is insufficient unless its contents actually support the statement.

Scale research and output to the request. A quick reading list can distinguish preliminary abstract-level leads; a decisive novelty claim or deep review requires stronger primary-source examination. Deliver the requested reading list, related-literature prose, comparison, or gap assessment; a detailed matrix is optional when it materially helps. See [outputs.md](references/outputs.md). Write scholarly finance prose that develops a connected economic argument rather than a catalog of abstracts or a research-status memo.

**If a pivotal claim cannot be verified, pursue its source; if still unresolved, say precisely what is missing and exclude it from the conclusion.** Neither plausible model reasoning nor citation formatting substitutes for reading evidence. No scripts, automated scores, mandatory forms, or reviewer cycles can certify source understanding; the agent must do the research and own its verification.
