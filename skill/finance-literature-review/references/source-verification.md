# Source Recovery, Reading, and Claim-Level Evidence

The main safeguard against invented literature is **traceability to source material actually examined**, combined with a genuine attempt to recover papers behind paywalls. An agent cannot make itself reliable by declaring it never hallucinates. Do the reading.

## Source-access states are claim-specific

- **Located / metadata only:** bibliographic identity or existence established; scholarly content unassessed.
- **Abstract examined:** may relay exact author-reported statements and numerical results as provisional *author reports*. Do not infer design details absent from the abstract or independently endorse causal validity, mechanisms, novelty, or boundary claims.
- **Relevant paper sections examined:** can make particular claims supported by those sections. The fact that a PDF was opened establishes nothing about pages or tables the agent never examined.
- **Design and evidence assessed:** can critically evaluate a paper's identifying assumptions and primary result **only after** inspecting the material methods, estimates/theorems, table notes, and relevant alternatives. Inspect appendix or robustness analysis when decisive to the inference.

These are **permissions for particular claims**, not prestige badges or a declaration that the whole paper has been verified. A document can support its research question while leaving a claimed mechanism unverified. “Full-text available” and “full-text read” mean different things.

## Recover full text rather than inventing it

For each pivotal but inaccessible paper, make a real, proportionate retrieval effort:

1. Check the publisher's article page, accepted manuscript, and legitimate open-access links; confirm author, title, DOI, year, and version.
2. Search the title/DOI and author names in the authors' sites, institutional repositories, SSRN, NBER, CEPR, RePEc, central-bank series, conference pages, and institutional working-paper collections.
3. Check OpenAlex, Unpaywall, Crossref, and citation links where supported. Try alternative open HTML and PDF versions and authorized university-library or connected-file access.
4. If a matching working-paper version is found, inspect its date and substantive differences from the published or cited version. Cite the version actually read and avoid silently transferring estimates or table numbers across versions.
5. If still blocked, request the actual PDF or institutional-access copy from the user for a pivotal paper, specifying why it matters. Continue on other sources when feasible. Do not build an originality or causal-identification conclusion around an inaccessible paper.

Search results, aggregators, author bios, abstracts, secondary summaries, and AI-generated explanations are **leads**. They cannot substitute for primary evidence when the claim concerns identification, estimates, theory, or contribution. Never invent a missing section, PDF, estimate, quote, page, or download action.

## Three flexible intellectual anchors

Locate these in the **actual paper**, regardless of paragraph or section numbering:

**Design/theory:** Economic question, institution or equilibrium mechanism, data and unit, comparison or variation, identification assumptions or model restrictions, and serious alternatives. Observe whether the design is observational, predictive, structural, theoretical, or credibly causal; do not presume every study has an exogenous shock.

**Evidence:** Read the principal table, figure, theorem, or model result, including its notes/definitions/uncertainty. Check sign, unit, baseline, horizon, sample, and any heterogeneity needed to interpret the finding. Verify the author's prose against the actual exhibit. Further results and appendix checks depend on which inferential claims are made.

**Contribution:** Read how the authors position themselves, then compare against the *actual closest previous papers*. Distinguish novelty claimed by authors from novelty corroborated through independent coverage. Papers can jointly originate theory, measurement, and empirical identification.

For each consequential output assertion, the agent must know the paper version and the supporting **page/section/table/equation locator**. For core novelty, identification, and result comparisons, disclose those real inspected locators in the deliverable or compact source notes. If the agent cannot furnish a locator because it did not inspect the source, the claim is **not eligible** for presentation as independently established. **A locator is valid only if the agent really examined its content.** Merely appending “Table 3” to a made-up claim is fabrication.

## Worked source-grounding illustration

[Khwaja and Mian (2008), “Tracing the Impact of Bank Liquidity Shocks: Evidence from an Emerging Market,” *American Economic Review*, 98(4):1413–1442](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.98.4.1413). An [accessible author-hosted published manuscript](https://khwaja.scholar.harvard.edu/files/asimkhwaja/files/khwaja_a_-tracing_the_impact_of_bank_liquidity_shocks_evidence_-_aer_2008.pdf) provides the relevant sections and exhibits.

- **Design source:** pp. 1414–1415 (introduction), Section II. The authors compare loan changes for the same multiple-bank borrowers across lenders experiencing different liquidity shocks after the 1998 nuclear tests in Pakistan; firm fixed effects absorb common borrower demand changes **under their identifying assumption**.
- **Evidence source:** Table 3, column 1, p. 1426, and its notes. The preferred firm-FE coefficient on the change in log bank liquidity is **0.60**; authors interpret a 1% larger liquidity decline as an additional 0.6% decline in the bank's lending to the same firm. The table describes the exact intensive-margin specification and its restricted multibank sample. Avoid conflating this coefficient with an aggregate firm borrowing effect.
- **Contribution source:** introduction, pp. 1414–1415. The paper studies both lender pass-through and firms' ability to substitute toward other lenders. The interpretation is conditional on the within-borrower design, the Pakistani shock, and the loan-level setting.

This example demonstrates **source → claim → inference**. It is grounded in named passages and a real exhibit. It does not authorize copying its conclusions into another paper, extrapolating to all firms, or claiming that a named table was checked without inspecting it.

**A known counterexample to avoid:** NBER Working Paper [32100](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32100) is *Does Pension Automatic Enrollment Increase Debt?*, a household-finance study. The repository's earlier literature-matrix template falsely labeled that number as a bank-capital and uninsured-deposit study. Do not reuse that fabricated row.

## End with source-to-synthesis reconciliation

Before delivery, read the **finished substantive claims** against the source passages/exhibits they purportedly summarize. Confirm paper identity and version, economically comparable outcomes and horizons, actual estimands, source-reported versus independently inferred conclusions, and whether the closest literature supports the novelty claim. If a claim fails, **fix it or omit it**. Do not create confidence through unsupported citation formatting, elaborate matrices, or review certificates.
