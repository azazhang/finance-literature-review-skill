# Optional Source-Grounded Finance Literature Matrix

Use this **only when structured cross-paper comparison helps the user's task**. The matrix supports critical synthesis; a completed row is neither proof of source access nor proof that a paper's inference is credible. Do not produce it automatically for a brief literature question or a related-literature paragraph.

## Suggested fields (adapt freely)

| Field | What belongs here |
|---|---|
| Paper and version | Accurate authors, title, year, DOI/link, published versus working-paper version, version date |
| Economic question and lineage | The financial object, mechanism, and potentially overlapping theoretical/empirical roles |
| Setting and data | Institutions, population, sample dates and filters, unit of observation |
| Design or theoretical restriction | Actual source of variation, identifying assumptions, equilibrium structure, or predictive design |
| Main result and economic magnitude | What the inspected table, figure, theorem, or model shows, with units and uncertainty where relevant |
| Evidence locator | **Exact inspected** source page/section, figure, table/column/notes, theorem/equation, or stable equivalent |
| Mechanism and alternatives | What authors propose, what tests support, what remains unseparated |
| Inferential boundary | Which interpretation is supported, what limitations actually constrain it |
| Link to user's question | Direct overlap, competing explanation, or genuine incremental inference |
| Access/verification status | Explicitly: metadata only, abstract-reported claim, relevant full-text section examined, or claim critically assessed |

For identification-sensitive comparisons, add separate fields for treatment variation, control group, fixed effects, clustering, exclusion restrictions, estimator, and uncertainty. For theory, replace econometric fields with assumptions, equilibrium conditions, propositions, and comparative statics. Use `not applicable` for irrelevant concepts and `not examined` where source access or reading was incomplete. Avoid arbitrary journal-quality tiers.

## A real, source-located illustration

**Khwaja and Mian (2008), “Tracing the Impact of Bank Liquidity Shocks: Evidence from an Emerging Market,” *American Economic Review* 98(4), 1413–1442.** [DOI and publisher](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.98.4.1413) · [Accessible author-hosted published manuscript](https://khwaja.scholar.harvard.edu/files/asimkhwaja/files/khwaja_a_-tracing_the_impact_of_bank_liquidity_shocks_evidence_-_aer_2008.pdf).

| Dimension | Extracted source-grounded information |
|---|---|
| Question | How bank-specific liquidity shocks affect lending and whether borrowers can offset the credit supply contraction |
| Setting | Pakistan, 1998 nuclear-test shock; loan-level panel of corporate borrowers |
| Design | Same firms borrowing from different banks exposed to different liquidity changes; first differences and firm fixed effects. See the **introduction (pp. 1414–1415)** and **Section II** for identifying assumptions |
| Baseline finding | **Table 3, column 1, p. 1426:** preferred firm-FE estimate on change in log bank liquidity is **0.60**; authors interpret a 1% larger bank-liquidity decline as 0.6% less lending from that bank to the same borrower |
| Econometric details | Table 3 and notes: intensive-margin multibank sample; firm fixed effects; standard errors clustered by bank |
| Interpretive boundary | Within-firm comparisons absorb shared borrower demand changes under the authors' assumptions; this coefficient measures a **lender-level pass-through**, not by itself firms' net aggregate borrowing response |
| Contribution | Examines bank lending and firm borrowing channels together; read pp. 1414–1415 and the later firm-borrowing analysis before evaluating the second channel |

The illustration is deliberately limited to claims supported by named locations. It cannot establish every conclusion about this paper or its intellectual priority. Each new row requires **actual inspection of its own source**; do not copy an earlier paper's assumptions or estimates into another row.

## Inaccessible paper example

If only an abstract was read, record a statement such as: “The abstract reports a reduction in outcome X; the underlying design, table, and inference have **not been examined**.” Do not invent an exact sample, mechanism test, or coefficient. For a pivotal comparison, follow [source recovery](../references/source-verification.md) and request the PDF if legitimate recovery fails. The paper remains provisional for critical synthesis.
